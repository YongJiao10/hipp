# Task-fMRI and BANDA: primary-source QC extension

Reviewed 2026-10-18. This is a focused source comparison, not a systematic prevalence estimate. It does not change production inclusion criteria.

## What the sources justify

The exact proposed M rule, the average of runwise mean relative RMS values being at most 0.5 mm, is **not established as a standard by this source set**. Finding one published use does not establish widespread adoption. Higher-profile and consortium sources use several distinct approaches: frame-level motion censoring, limits on contaminated data, image inspection, and sometimes an explicit tSNR threshold. These should not be conflated.

Every participant passing M does not by itself invalidate a threshold. It establishes that this particular gate excludes nobody in the available sample; it does not show that all scans are artifact-free. A temporal mean can hide brief motion bursts, and an average across runs can hide a worse run. The original HCP QC study shows why the scale matters: across 2,207 task/rest runs, mean relative motion averaged 0.093 mm, with a between-run SD of 0.029 mm and a 95th percentile of 0.15 mm. A 0.5 mm mean-relative-motion gate is remote from that distribution. The paper also assessed frame exceedance percentages and DVARS, rather than relying solely on the temporal mean. [Marcus et al., NeuroImage 2013, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3845379/)

| Tozzi et al., **Nature Medicine 2024** | Excludes scans with more than 25% of volumes showing significant FD, alongside image review. | Hack, Jubeir et al., **Nature Mental Health 2025** | A GoNoGo precision-medicine trial specifies less than 25% of time points censored for FD and tSNR at least 50, following motion correction, 128-s high-pass filtering and MNI registration. | Explicit numeric tSNR exclusion does exist in a recent higher-profile clinical task study. [Methods: Brain imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589093/) |
| Garvert et al., **Nature Neuroscience 2023** | A hippocampal cognitive-map task study computes FD/DVARS and annotates frames exceeding 0.5 mm FD or 1.5 standardized DVARS. Its GLMs include FD, six motion parameters, six aCompCor components and four cosine regressors. | This is a hippocampus-focused task example. Annotation is not proof that those frames were censored: the reported GLM inclusion list must be described accurately. No universal tSNR gate can be inferred from it. [Methods: fMRI preprocessing and analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01283-x) |

The task-method comparison by Jones et al. analyzed eight public datasets spanning eleven tasks. Modest censoring often helped, but no method or threshold was consistently best. Their tested fixed FD0.9 threshold would censor less than 1% in most datasets and substantially more in some others. This supports checking actual temporal motion burden without claiming that a desired rejection percentage establishes validity. It is not a reason to start a new optimization project. [Jones et al., Aperture Neuro 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9506314/)

## Fully specified rules available for the five-dataset comparison

**Secondary FD comparison: Power FD>0.5 mm in no more than 25% of each run's frames.** Tozzi et al.'s supplementary QC paragraph rejects scans exceeding 25% significant-FD volumes (PDF physical page 5, printed page 4). Its fMRIPrep reports specify FD>0.5 mm or standardized DVARS>1.5 as outliers (physical pages 53, 57, 61, 65 and 66). Combining the FD amplitude with the QC proportion is an inference across these statements; label the comparison the paper's **FD-motion component**, rather than a reproduction of its entire pipeline. The QC paragraph names FD, so this comparison counts FD exceedances, not the union with DVARS. Exactly 25% passes; a frame exactly at 0.5 mm is not above threshold. No surrounding-frame padding is stated. The paper removes three initial volumes before preprocessing; project comparisons use the frames represented by their declared analysis inputs. Its task-free connectivity comes from task-GLM residuals, filtered and concatenated across tasks, rather than a separate resting-state acquisition. [Supplementary Information](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41591-024-03057-9/MediaObjects/41591_2024_3057_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)

The supplement was parsed in one batch with py314 `opendataloader_pdf.convert`, producing [Markdown](../refs/Tozzi2024_NatureMedicine_SupplementaryInformation.md) and [JSON](../refs/Tozzi2024_NatureMedicine_SupplementaryInformation.json); JSON page metadata confirms these locations. fMRIPrep distinguishes Power `framewise_displacement` from Jenkinson `rmsd`, and documents the FD0.5/standardized-DVARS1.5 default outlier rule. Existing FSL relative RMS is therefore a separate metric. [Official fMRIPrep documentation](https://fmriprep.org/en/1.5.5/outputs.html)

**Supplementary RMS motion comparison: relative RMS>0.25 mm in no more than 20% of each run's frames.** Hearne et al., *Activity flow underlying abnormalities in brain activations and cognition in schizophrenia*, **Science Advances 7 (2021), eabf2513**, exclude a participant when any functional run exceeds this proportion. Motion exclusions were six healthy controls and twelve schizophrenia participants. The final cohort was 93 controls and 36 patients. The study analyzes working-memory activations and task-general FC from rest plus balloon-risk, stop-signal and task-switching runs. It applies 24 motion and 20 physiological regressors; high-motion resting-state frames receive spike regressors, and task nuisance terms enter the task GLM. It does not impose an additional subject-mean RMS0.5 criterion. [Primary Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8279516/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abf2513)

Keane et al., *Increased whole-brain functional heterogeneity in psychosis during rest and task*, **NeuroImage: Clinical 43 (2024), 103630**, reuse the RMS0.25/20% rule for their UCLA replication combining one rest scan with three task scans. This sample retains 93 controls, 36 schizophrenia patients and 35 ADHD participants. Its separate HCP Early Psychosis sample uses a different FD-based preprocessing protocol; do not attribute the UCLA RMS rule to that HCP sample. This is a repeated methodological lineage, not two independent estimates of field-wide prevalence. [Primary Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11225660/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nicl.2024.103630)

These rules supply two executable published choices with run-level burden limits. Applying the same motion formula and proportion to all five datasets is a project implementation decision. Count a participant as passing only when every run declared for that analysis branch passes; report PDC sessions separately before any person-level aggregation. This literature extraction does not itself censor time series or change production inclusion.

## BANDA-specific evidence and the new harmonized release

The actual protocol reference is **Siless et al., NeuroImage: Clinical 26 (2020), 102242**, not NeuroImage 213:116703. It describes voxelwise tSNR averaged over voxels and then runs, and motion assessments including FD. This is direct evidence that BANDA investigators used tSNR for quality assessment; the described quality-summary sections do not supply the proposed subject-mean relative RMS0.5 exclusion rule. [Sections 6.1–6.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7184183/)

The companion task overview explicitly states that only minimal denoising was used for its descriptive presentation and that future analyses should consider additional practices including frame censoring. It also recommends task-performance criteria; those recommendations concern task analysis, not the BANDA resting-state runs currently measured here. [Hubbard et al., NeuroImage: Clinical 27 (2020), 102240](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213158220300772)

**HARMONY** is unusually relevant because it pools HCP-BANDA, HCP-PDC, HCP-DES and HCP-DAM. It uses common HCP-style preprocessing extended with surface review, ICA-FIX, MSMAll, Reclean and temporal ICA. It reports tSNR and DVARS and provides participant-level QC to permit analysis-specific inclusion criteria. It does not turn this project's M rule into a common standard. Importantly, its verified status is a **bioRxiv preprint posted 1 July 2026**, DOI 10.64898/2026.06.26.732748; it is useful cohort documentation, not peer-reviewed authority. [Resource description and QC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13345329/), [PubMed publication type](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42427512/)

## Motion definitions used in the executed comparison

For HCP-style `Movement_Regressors.txt`, the first six columns are translations in mm followed by rotations in degrees; the final six are their derivatives. `Movement_Regressors_dt.txt` has means and linear trends removed. This is documented directly by HCP pipeline author Matthew Glasser. [HCP author clarification](https://www.mail-archive.com/hcp-users%40humanconnectome.org/msg00002.html)

An explicitly labeled Power FD trace can therefore be derived from successive differences of the **first six original columns**:

`FD[t] = sum(abs(delta_translation_mm)) + 50 * pi / 180 * sum(abs(delta_rotation_degrees))`.

The 50-mm rotational radius is part of the FD convention described by the BANDA protocol above. Use an explicit first-frame convention, do not accidentally differentiate the derivative columns, and do not label this trace as the existing FSL relative RMS. Marcus et al. note that their MCFLIRT relative motion is approximately half Power FD; this is an empirical approximation, not an exact conversion for these data.

This measurement needs only existing small motion tables. It can show whether all-pass M masks short high-motion periods. A published frame threshold and run/subject exclusion rule must be chosen explicitly before interpreting a pass count; raw FD should not be silently substituted for respiratory-filtered FD. This review does not apply a new exclusion rule or reclean the fMRI time series.

## Decision boundary

The primary target is hippocampal surface-vertex QC. Mean RMS0.5 is the retained supplementary motion candidate. R20 is excluded from further consideration; completed R20 and Tozzi-informed F25 measurements are research comparisons; they do not identify local unusable vertices. The [surface-QC decision](tsnr_task_rest_qc_justification.md) retains surface T25 and direct published surface-mapping coverage as local comparisons. The [five-dataset report](qc_five_datasets.md) labels surface versus native-volume results. No production criterion has changed.

## Audit of proposed hippocampal surface rules

Reviewed 2026-10-19 against the packaged primary papers and their official examples. **Prioritize surface-to-fMRI coverage and vertexwise signal quality; retain the existing midthickness sampling and mesh.** These papers do not require a new mesh or histology-processing pipeline for the present fMRI QC comparison.

| Proposed rule | What the primary source actually supports | Decision for this project |
|---|---|---|
| DeKraker: mandatory midthickness sampling eliminates partial-volume contamination | HippUnfold constructs inner, outer and midthickness surfaces. The official rsfMRI tutorial samples the midthickness surface after transforming it into functional space. This is a concrete sampling choice, not a guarantee of zero partial-volume contamination or an exclusive rule for all modalities. | Keep the existing midthickness sampling; assess actual functional coverage separately. |
| DeKraker: fMRI must use 2-mm / 419 vertices and must never use 0.5-mm / 7,262 vertices | The MRI Methods use 2-mm spacing / 419 vertices to match lower-resolution fMRI. They do not prohibit a denser mesh. Kember explicitly analyzes 0.5-mm / 7,262-vertex surfaces, with 2-mm-FWHM smoothing. | Do not change the existing mesh to obtain a QC pass rate. Treat density as an analysis choice, not an exclusion criterion. |
| DeKraker: local 10-vertex / 4-SD filtering, dilated by two vertices | This appears under **Histology** and addresses excess stain and missing-area interfaces. The paper specifies a ten-vertex **radius**, not a fixed ten-neighbor sample, and subsequently fills missing data by interpolation/extrapolation. | Skip for the present fMRI QC. It is not a published fMRI vertex-rejection rule. |
| DeKraker: 25-layer profile shifts limited to 25% thickness | This also appears under **Histology**, aligning microstructural depth profiles against a global profile and addressing tissue-segmentation/modal misalignment. | Skip; it does not describe BOLD-surface QC. |
| DeKraker: mandatory buffer masks at the anterior and posterior poles | The Discussion warns that native-space edges contain limited data and can show interpolation artifacts. It provides no buffer width or automatic pole-exclusion rule. | Inspect the poles on the existing QC maps; do not add an unreported fixed mask. |
| Kember: exclude subjects with at least 20 mm² of improperly interpolated surface | The QC Methods do exclude subjects for at least 20 mm² of poor interpolation caused by insufficient overlap of hippocampal surfaces with preprocessed fMRI gray-matter voxels. The text does not specify whether the area is evaluated per hemisphere or summed bilaterally, nor a numeric rule for classifying a vertex as improperly interpolated. | Highest-priority local QC comparison. Report unsupported area on each native surface; distinguish an explicit project implementation from details not stated in the paper. |

DeKraker primary evidence: [Nature Methods paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02783-3); packaged [Histology and MRI Methods](../refs/DeKraker_2025_HippoMaps/DeKraker_2025_HippoMaps.md#histology), Markdown lines 1247–1264, physical PDF page 13 (JSON paragraph IDs 728 and 733); [surface mapping](../refs/DeKraker_2025_HippoMaps/DeKraker_2025_HippoMaps.md#surface-mapping), lines 1276–1280, page 14 (IDs 744–745); edge caution at Markdown line 1061, page 8 (ID 685). The [official rsfMRI tutorial](https://hippomaps.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/MRI-3T-rsfMRI.html) uses midthickness surfaces and explicitly calls its mapping code an example requiring customization. It also computes and displays vertexwise temporal mean/SD without a tSNR25 exclusion gate. The displayed checkpoint values are near zero, so this example supplies no transferable numeric calibration.

Kember primary evidence: [Nature Communications Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74572-1#Sec8); packaged [precision-mapping and QC Methods](../refs/Kember_2026_Hippocampus_Longitudinal_Axis/Kember_2026_Hippocampus_Longitudinal_Axis.md), Markdown lines 158–172, physical PDF pages 17–18 (JSON paragraph IDs 262 and 270). The published [analysis notebook](https://github.com/JonahKember/posterior_hippocampus_development/blob/main/manuscript_analyses.ipynb) operates on derived analysis tables and does not implement the interpolation QC. The [mapping source](https://github.com/JonahKember/precision_mapping/blob/main/hipp_mapping/mapping.py) maps with trilinear interpolation and smooths the result; it does not resolve the 20-mm² QC definition. Its separate 25-mm² cluster-cleaning parameter concerns functional-system labels and must not be mistaken for the interpolation QC threshold.
